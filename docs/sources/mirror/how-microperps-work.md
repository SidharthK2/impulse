How MicroPerps WorkCopy as Markdown
On Mirror, every spot market starts with a perp with 3x leverage, scaling to 100x as the market matures. This is all thanks to a new innovation we’ve developed that we call the Micro Perpetual, or MicroPerp for short.
MicroPerps allow us to go from perps on hundreds of assets maintained by gatekeepers to perps on millions of assets maintained by no one, making perps permissionless in the original sense of the word.
Today, exchanges like Binance and Hyperliquid can only list perpetuals for a few hundred out of the millions of assets that exist on-chain and in the real world. Even after listing an asset, if its liquidity subsequently deteriorates, they have to delist it. But why is this the case, and could we question their assumptions to engineer a truly permissionless perps venue? After all, isn’t the point of DeFi that we don’t have gatekeepers telling us what we can and can’t trade?
## Truly Permissionless Listing
MicroPerps can extend the magic of leverage and shorting to the long tail of assets by taking the exchange out of the listing decision.
The core reason existing perpetuals exchanges have to be conservative about what they list is that when a trader’s position goes negative, the exchange is the first line of defense to take over that bad debt. On Binance this manifests as an insurance fund, and on Hyperliquid it’s their HLP vault, but in either case the exchange takes a hit if they list an illiquid asset that results in a cascade of insolvency. The reason for this is largely historical: exchanges have had a means of socializing losses for some time via so-called “automated deleveraging” (ADL), but this has traditionally been used as a last line of defense, and virtually never as the sole means of settling bad debt.
Mirror starts by questioning this assumption: what would a system look like that solely relied on ADL, without putting the exchange on the hook for bad debt at all? The result is unintuitive because initially it seems like you’re worsening the experience. After all, isn’t it strictly better for the user if the exchange tries to cover bad debt before socializing losses? But consider the price of this backstop: In order for the exchange to be willing to cover losses on a market, it will necessarily have to impose stringent listing criteria, and permissionless listing will be out of the question entirely. If you can get the exchange out of the liquidation stack, and invest in completely re-architecting the risk engine from the ground up, what you get is an exchange where perp listing can be completely permissionless, and start in a market’s most nascent stages (i.e. an exchange that’s first-to-list for everything). This means perp markets can be initialized on any asset, no matter how early or illiquid, with virtually no impact to the trading experience. The end result is an exchange where millions of perps can trade, virtually unbounded, rather than just a few hundred.
Concretely, consider a MicroPerp for a highly-illiquid memecoin as an extreme example. The MicroPerp can exist from that memecoin’s inception, but you may not want to trade it initially because large swings in the spot price could result in liquidation or socialized losses. The question is: When is the right time to trade such an asset? Modern exchanges like Hyperliquid and Binance make this decision for you: They don’t list things until the risk, as determined by them, is low enough that they think you’ll be “safe” (and remember it’s really themselves they’re protecting from losses, not you). With MicroPerps, because the exchange bears no risk upon listing a new perp, we can list the perp immediately and let the market decide when it’s safe enough to trade. Pioneering traders can speculate as early in the lifecycle of an asset as they want, and accept the increased risk of liquidation or socialized losses. Meanwhile, less-risk-tolerant traders can simply wait to play in a market until it has matured (but again it’s up to them when that point has been reached, and everyone will have a different assessment). It may seem small, but this is what’s required to unlock fully-permissionless listing of perps. And if you unlock that, you can create new markets, like listing a perp for every venture-backed company long before it’s gone public, listing a perp for every prediction market automatically, listing a perp for every home, listing a perp for every social profile on the internet, etc...
Simply put: MicroPerps can extend the magic of leverage and shorting to the long tail of assets, in much the same way bonding curves greatly expanded the market for small-cap spot assets. Of course, the most illiquid ones will be quite risky to trade, but for the first time that will be up to the market to decide rather than centralized gatekeepers. And isn’t that ultimately what DeFi is all about?
## Permissionless User-Managed Markets
Because MicroPerps can function on arbitrarily illiquid price feeds (and even on no price feed at all for pre-launch markets), they enable free and fully permissionless user-managed markets, i.e. markets that are deployed and managed by third parties. Today Hyperliquid’s HIP-3 protocol requires a ~$40M up-front stake that can be slashed, as well as an opaque whitelisting process, in order to deploy a third-party market to their frontend. This buffer is required for them precisely because the exchange is on the hook for losses if a market malfunctions. By eliminating the exchange from the liquidation stack, as MicroPerps do, we can make deploying a market cost nothing, while also making the process of deploying a market truly permissionless.
On Mirror, deploying a market essentially means you are the one providing the oracle for that market and setting the metadata for your market (e.g. what fees are charged), as opposed to the Mirror platform itself providing the oracle & metadata, as is the case with Mirror-owned asset categories like Attention Markets. In exchange for this service you earn 90% of the fees associated with your market. This provides third parties an incentive to provide a strong oracle, as well as an incentive to provide ample liquidity for their markets.
Perhaps the most beautiful thing about the system is that creating a market will be totally self-service and cost nothing. Mirror holds the view that it should be up to the users to decide what markets are reliable and liquid enough to trade, not the exchange, and MicroPerps make this vision achievable for the first time.
## Mathematically-Guaranteed Solvency
Taking the exchange out of the listing decision means first taking the exchange off the hook for bad debt. The best way to do that is to ensure bad debt can never exist in the first place.
In order for a perps exchange to function without someone whitelisting markets, it must be the case that there is no bad debt lurking in any market at any given time. That is, each market is mathematically guaranteed to be “solvent” immediately after every state transition. This is a major break from how centralized exchanges treat risk, as they often allow insolvencies to persist while they liquidate assets or let the risk engine catch up.
Mirror achieves this by running solvency checks before every single trade and every single price tick, and running ADL atomically within the transaction itself if anyone has gone negative. Whereas exchanges typically have dedicated risk engines that run as a separate process from matching, Mirror bakes the risk engine and matching engine into a single monolithic core that runs atomically on every transaction. This is the only way that solvency can be guaranteed atomically upon each operation, without requiring an exchange’s capital to act as a backstop.
With the matching and risk engines consolidated into a single thread, you might wonder how we achieve scale. Even if a single thread can achieve over 300k steady-state transactions per second, as it can in Mirror’s implementation, we still need to be able to shard markets across threads in order to reach our goal of hosting millions of markets. This is achieved by keeping all markets in isolated margin by default. When two markets are isolated, and when ADL is run as described above on every transaction, we can shard markets and still be assured that the system as a whole is solvent as long as each individual market is solvent before and after every transaction.
Keeping all markets in isolated margin to start is also desirable because it prevents liquidations in one market from impacting others. As markets mature, including them in cross or portfolio margin is a simple flag-flip. In this case, however, discretion is required because including a highly-illiquid market in cross or portfolio margin could cause socialized losses to participants in the other markets in the set.
## Max Leverage on MicroPerps
MicroPerps on highly-illiquid markets, such as Attention Tokens and external Ethereum and Solana tokens, start initially with 3x leverage the moment a spot market is created. As the market matures, leverage increases according to a combination of volume and depth, eventually reaching 100x leverage once the market has fully matured.
We do not share the exact graduation criteria here, but we note that in the limit as a MicroPerp’s volume and depth continue to increase, it essentially becomes a normal perp with high leverage, with a standard mark price and funding rate.
## Bootstrapping a MicroPerp Market
MicroPerps bootstrap largely like traditional perps do, with minimal adjustment for users and virtually no change in the UX. The steps are as follows:
Initially, the perp market will allow 3x leverage but will have no orders in it. At this point the funding rate will be at its maximum base rate, strongly incentivizing short liquidity to get things started.
As the market matures, the funding rate stabilizes at more reasonable levels and the amount of leverage allowed on the perp market organically increases over time.
Whenever insolvencies occur, whether early on or otherwise, the system’s advanced ADL system neutralizes them atomically, within the transaction that caused them, mathematically guaranteeing solvency after every event.
If the market never matures or stops being active, any active perp positions continue to exist, paying the funding rate determined at the last tick. The exchange can tolerate millions of inactive perp markets with virtually no cost to the system.
The above seems simple, and yet it enables perps on highly-illiquid markets for the first time, and can scale to a platform with millions of perps listed instead of hundreds.
## Other Applications
We mentioned how MicroPerps can enable leverage and shorting on any asset, without relying on a gatekeeper to whitelist markets. Below we flesh out some of the applications concretely and how they will work:
A mirror of the stock market. This is the most obvious application. Every equity with a price can simply have a MicroPerp on Mirror. Critically, the fact that they don’t trade at night or on the weekends is no problem because MicroPerps are robust to gaps in the price feed. Users would not have to wait for listing either. Pre-IPO markets work just the same, with pricing starting whenever the market opens. We plan to automatically have a MicroPerp not just for every asset listed on the NYSE, but also international exchanges including the SSE, Euronext, the TSE, and many more. Importantly, we won’t just list the most liquid assets; MicroPerps allow us to list the full universe of names, irrespective of liquidity, on day one.
All VC-backed private companies. Because MicroPerps are robust in the absence of a price feed, they enable the automatic listing of perps for companies that do not even have a valid spot market yet. We initially plan to take a whitelist of venture capitalists and automatically list a perp market for each of the companies they have backed. This is semi-permissioned, but still a much wider net than any other exchange has ever been able to offer.
A mirror of the commodities market. No different than the stock market above. Any commodity can automatically have a MicroPerp, regardless of how frequently or how reliably it ticks. We plan to automatically have a MicroPerp for every asset listed on the CME.
A perp for every on-chain token. Similar to listing a perp for every stock and commodity, Mirror can have a perp for every on-chain asset listed totally permissionlessly. We plan to automatically create a perp market for every asset on Uniswap (for Ethereum) and every asset on pump.fun and other top DEXes (for Solana, Robinhood Chain, and more). If successful, Mirror can become a literal “mirror” of every on-chain market, accessible in one place.
Prediction markets with leverage. MicroPerps uniquely unlock the ability to add leverage to prediction markets. There is no reason why every prediction market on Polymarket and Kalshi can’t have a mirrored MicroPerp on Mirror for those who want to amplify their returns.
Real-estate speculation (Zillow Perps). Imagine if you could speculate on the value of any basket of homes in real time. Real estate prices are less liquid than traditional equities and commodities, but that's not a problem for MicroPerps. Every listing on Zillow can attach to a unique MicroPerp, and "baskets" of home prices can be aggregated to create an oracle price for every city in the world. This means you can directly long or short baskets of real-estate prices like all homes in SF or all homes in Hong Kong. With time, we think this can become among the most liquid ways to speculate on the value of real-estate.
These are just some examples of what’s possible with the technology we’ve developed.
